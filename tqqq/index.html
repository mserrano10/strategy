1. Executive Summary & Core Mandate

The 2-Bullet Asymmetric Leveraged Equity Strategy is a rules-based, tail-risk exploitation engine. It converts macro equity market panics into non-linear portfolio compounding by systematically deploying cash into 3x daily leveraged NASDAQ-100 exposure (TQQQ) during structural drawdowns.

To eliminate single-asset liquidation risk and smooth multi-decade equity curves, the strategy is constructed as a 90/10 Hybrid Portfolio Architecture: 90% of overall net worth resides in core broad-market indexing (S&P 500 / SPY), while 10% is dedicated to the 2-Bullet Asymmetric Engine.

Core Strategic Directives
  • Hybrid Allocation: 90% Broad Market (S&P 500) / 10% Satellite Asymmetric Engine (0% intermediate rebalancing).
  • Engine Capital Split: 50% Bullet 1 (-50% Drawdown Trigger) / 50% Bullet 2 (-80% Drawdown Trigger).
  • Dry Powder Integrity: Uninvested satellite cash must reside exclusively in Treasury MMFs or Ultra-Short T-Bills. Equities or SPY derivatives are strictly prohibited as dry powder proxies due to crash impairment.
  • Risk Control Cap: Capping the engine at 10% isolates daily derivative leverage and overnight liquidation risks while adding 4%–6% in compound annual alpha.

2. Institutional Execution Algorithm

STEP 1 Portfolio Partitioning & Reserve Custody Insulating core wealth from tail-risk
Allocate total account capital into two un-rebalanced sleeves:
  • S&P 500 Core (90%): Fully invested in SPY for perpetual broad-market compounding.
  • Satellite Engine (10%): Split 50/50 into Bullet 1 Cash (5%) and Bullet 2 Cash (5%) held in Treasury MMF.
STEP 2 High-Water Mark (HWM) & Trigger Activation Systematic entry execution
Continuously track TQQQ's active High-Water Mark (PATH).
  • Bullet 1 Trigger: Activates when TQQQ drops ≥ 50% from PATH.
  • Bullet 2 Trigger: Activates when TQQQ drops ≥ 80% from that same locked PATH.
STEP 3 Volume-Weighted Tranche Execution (3-Month Cadence) Averaging down without falling-knife risk
Deploy active Bullet capital across a 90-day window upon trigger breach:
  • Month 1: Deploy 50% of active bullet capital.
  • Month 2: Deploy 25% of active bullet capital.
  • Month 3: Deploy remaining 25% of active bullet capital.
STEP 4 State Lock & Limit Exit Execution Disciplined profit realization
Upon establishing the cycle floor (Pfloor), set a limit sell order at 10 × Pfloor.
  • State Lock Protocol: No secondary entries are permitted while a position is active.
  • Full Rollover: Upon target execution, 100% of realized satellite cash is rolled forward into Treasury MMF for the next macro cycle.

1. Liquidation Risk Isolation: ProShares explicitly warns that a single-day drop of >33.3% in the Nasdaq-100 results in a -100% loss ($0 NAV). Capping the engine at 10% guarantees that a Black Swan liquidation event leaves 90% of total wealth completely intact inside the S&P 500 core.

2. Dry Powder Impairment Protection: Keeping Bullet 2 in an S&P 500 index fund rather than MMF reduces final strategy wealth by ~40%. When TQQQ touches its -80% trigger, broad equities are down -25% to -50%, forcing forced liquidation of equities at market troughs.

Keep the S&P 500 as the core. Use a small pocket, and only a small pocket, to bet that the Nasdaq bounces back after a crash.

The idea

Keep about 90% in a plain S&P 500 core, and leave it there. Hold about 10% as a pocket that buys the 3x fund only after a crash. The pocket is a bet that the Nasdaq bounces back. Do not switch the core to QQQ. The pocket is already that Nasdaq bet, and a QQQ core would put the rest of the portfolio into the same risk.

How to read it
  • If it is timing luck. The pocket started buying on three dates and finished two round trips. A short record can look like a rule when it is only a couple of rebounds.
  • If it depends on the era. This path sits in the years after 2014, and it includes the rebound from the 2022 buys. The file does not show a slow recovery, and it does not show another interest-rate backdrop.
  • If it is just the Nasdaq beating the S&P 500. The pocket is that bet, kept small, and taken only after a crash. Leave the core in the S&P 500. Switching the core to QQQ would add Nasdaq risk on top of the pocket, and this file does not measure that switch.
  • If it depends on rebuilt data from before 2010. Set that reading aside for this path. The lab uses the listed fund. The earlier table is a different model, with its own pre-2010 reconstruction.
  • If you cannot sit through the drops. On this path the mix fell 35.8%, against 33.7% for the S&P 500 alone. The memo already caps the pocket at 10%, because a very bad single day in the Nasdaq-100 can wipe out the 3x fund, and the pocket with it.

Before you read the tables

1. This is not 37 tests. The 37 start years overlap heavily and move together. In the real TQQQ era (13 starts, 2010 to 2022) the pocket beats the S&P 500 core in 13 of 13 starts, but all 13 rest on the same 3 crash episodes (late 2018, March 2020, 2022), and every start from 2010 to 2018 makes exactly the same trades. The 24 starts from 1986 to 2009 use a modelled 3x Nasdaq-100 fund, because TQQQ did not exist. They rest on 4 distinct modelled crash episodes, plus 4 later re-buys that happen only because the modelled fund never got back to its 2000 peak. Counting 37 results as 37 pieces of evidence overstates the case.

2. Most of the wins are leverage and lucky starting points, not the rule, and the rule did not pass its own test. With an S&P 500 core the pocket ends above the core in 25 of 37 starts, but 4 are close calls, so the clear wins are 21 (15 to 25 depending on the end date). Holding the 3x fund from day one with no timing beats the S&P 500 core in 32 of 37 starts and the QQQ core in 21 of 37, while the pocket beats that held fund in only 16 of 37 (6 of 13 from 2010): most of "the pocket beats its core" is 3x leverage in a long bull market, not the pocket rule. The biggest results are day-one artefacts: the 3x fund was already more than 50% below its high, or the first buy came within 10 trading days (15 of 37 starts, an arbitrary cut-off; up to 9.9 times the S&P 500 core). Without them, in the 10 pre-2010 starts left the pocket ends below its S&P 500 core in 9 and below its QQQ core in all 10, only 0.1% to 14.0% above a control that keeps the 10% in T-bills. The rule failed the pre-registered robustness test, and falls from 6 to 5 of 8 events without the 1997 to 2000 bubble. A variant that buys earlier (B) passed the pre-registered wording, but it was one of three tried, it also falls to 5 of 8 without that bubble, and it beat held 3x in only 6 of 8 events with a negative average. B is a hypothesis, not a rule or a result.

3. The pocket is 10% only on day one, the loss is not capped at $10,000, and the end values flatter the real-era starts. After every sale the whole pocket is re-bet, so it grows with winnings and by the end date is up to 91% of the portfolio. A 3x fund can lose 80% to 99%: real TQQQ fell 81.8% in 2022 and the modelled fund fell 99.98% from 2000 to 2009. The -75.8% fall (2009 start, 2021-11-19 to 2022-12-28, against -33.7% for the S&P 500 alone) is the worst only for the pocket with an S&P 500 core. With a QQQ core the pocket fell 85.8%, pocket version B fell 85.9% (S&P 500 core) and 89.8% (QQQ core), and holding the 3x fund from day one fell 96.5%. For the 2010 to 2018 starts the pocket sold everything at 10 times the low on 2026-05-26 and has held about $280,000 in T-bills since (24.5% to 48.4% of the portfolio with an S&P 500 core), so the end values are measured just after a sale near a peak, with the pocket out of the market. Results are hypothetical, before taxes and trading costs, and every sale at 10 times the low is a taxable gain.

Plain words

Core. The 90% of the money that is simply bought once and never touched: the S&P 500 (S&P core) or the Nasdaq-100 through QQQ (QQQ core). "Core held alone" means the whole $100,000 in that index.

Pocket. The other 10%, kept in T-bills until a crash. After the fund the pocket buys has fallen far enough from its high, the pocket buys that fund in steps and sells everything when it has risen to 10 times its lowest close since the crash began. After each sale the whole pocket is split in two equal halves again, so it starts at 10% and grows with winnings (or shrinks with losses).

QQQ core. The same pocket, but with the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) as the 90% core instead of the S&P 500.

Day-one artefact. A result that mostly reflects the starting date, not the rule: the 3x fund was already more than 50% below its high on the start date, or the pocket's first buy came within 10 trading days. The pocket then simply holds a 3x fund bought after a crash had already happened. Marked artefact_flag in the tables. The 10-day cut-off is arbitrary; at 60 days two more starts (2020, 2022) would be marked and both are wins.

Pre-registered. The pass or fail rules for the formal test were written down and frozen (on 2026-10-22) before any test was run, so they could not be adjusted after seeing the results. The start-year tables were made after the fact and are not pre-registered.

Held 3x. The same 10% put into the 3x fund on day one and never sold or rebalanced. It shows what the 3x leverage alone does with no crash timing.

Close call. An ending value within 7.6% of the comparison. Treat it as a tie.

Control. 90% core plus 10% T-bills that are never invested.

How to read the full set
  • If it is timing luck. A day-one mark means that cell mostly reflects the start date. The 3x fund was already more than 50% below its high, or the first buy came within 10 trading days.
  • If it depends on the era. A modelled mark on the year means the 3x fund is modelled. Compare start years on growth per year, not on ending dollars.
  • If it is just the Nasdaq beating the S&P 500. In one row, compare a QQQ core with an S&P core on growth per year or the ratio to that core.
  • If it depends on modelled data. Read the first caveat. The start years overlap, and counting each row as a separate test overstates the case.
  • If you cannot sit through the drops. Use the worst-drop view. Ending dollars do not show the fall. Read the third caveat before treating an ending value as the whole story.
